Results for the Brodeur et al. (2024) data by pre-registration status, on their sample: following their replication files, we drop articles in \textit{Experimental Economics} and studies that are not experiments, and keep every test statistic with a recorded pre-registration status and $z$-statistic.
A study counts as pre-registered if it was registered before its trial end date.
De-rounding: uniform, following Brodeur et al. (2016). Where the coefficient and standard error are reported, each is replaced by an independent uniform draw from its rounding interval, and any other $t$-statistic gets uniform noise at its last digit.
We also drop every $t$-statistic computed from a coefficient and standard error whose rounding unit in $t$-units, $\kappa$, exceeds 0.3; the rule depends only on how results are printed, so the sample is the same for every de-rounding choice.
All $p$-values and breakdown statistics are averages over 10 independent de-roundings.
Columns headed $J_0$ report $p$-values and breakdown statistics $\widehat{B}$ of the projection test with $J=40$.
We did not collect effective sample sizes for this sample, so Med.\ $\nu$ and $\Delta_\nu$ are not reported.
We form $\mathcal{X}$ using a grid of 3000 elements evenly spaced between $\pm 6.5$ plus the scalar 9999.
Discretization incurs error $\mathcal{E}(L,\delta)=0.00063$, which is already accounted for in $\widehat{B}$ and the $p$-value.
The discretization was chosen to be made of round numbers and result in a small error.
EKW $p$-values are for the tests of Elliott et al. (2022): the least concave majorant test (LCM) and Fisher's test (Fisher) on $p$-values in $(0,0.15]$, the density discontinuity test at 0.05 (Disc.), the binomial test on $[0.04,0.05]$ (Binomial), and the Cox and Shi (2023) histogram-based tests on $(0,0.15]$ with 15 bins for monotonicity (CS1) and for 2-monotonicity with bounds (CS2B).
As in the input files of Brodeur et al. (2024), these $p$-values are $2(1-\Phi(|t|))$ in single precision.
Kudrin (2025) $p$-values are for his KS-type test ($T_\infty$), histogram-based test ($T_H$) and hybrid histogram-based test ($T_H^{\mathrm{hybrid}}$), using the sinc kernel with $\kappa=3$ and, for $T_H$ and $T_H^{\mathrm{hybrid}}$, the points $0.01,0.02,\ldots,0.15$.
All three cluster by article: $T_H$ and $T_H^{\mathrm{hybrid}}$ use cluster-robust covariance matrices and $T_\infty$ uses an article-level bootstrap.
$^\dagger$The deconvolution estimate already satisfies the hybrid test's shape restrictions, so $T_H^{\mathrm{hybrid}}=T_H$.
Min is the smallest of the averaged $p$-values in each group of tests.
The projection test and $T_\infty$ use 1000 bootstrap repetitions.
Computation used osqp package in R by Stellato et al. (2024). Data: Brodeur et al. (2024).
Transparency R package available at: \url{https://github.com/sfaridan/When_is_p_hacking_detectable}
