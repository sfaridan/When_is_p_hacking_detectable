\footnotesize
\textit{Notes:} $p$-values for the null hypothesis of no selective reporting in the data of Brodeur et al.\ (2020), by research design (RCT: randomized controlled trials; IV: instrumental variables; DID: difference-in-differences; RDD: regression discontinuity), with one row for each treatment of rounding.
\textit{None}: each $t$-statistic is the ratio of the reported coefficient and standard error, and reported $t$-statistics are used as printed.
\textit{Unif.}: the coefficient and the standard error are each replaced by an independent uniform draw from its rounding interval, and reported $t$-statistics receive uniform noise at their last digit; $p$-values are averages over 200 draws.
$s_{\min}$: each coefficient is divided by the smallest standard error consistent with the reported one, and reported $t$-statistics are used as printed (Section 5.2); this treatment is deterministic.
$s_{\min}$+noise: each coefficient, plus uniform noise at its last digit, is divided by the smallest standard error consistent with the reported one, and reported $t$-statistics receive uniform noise at their last digit (Section 5.2); $p$-values are averages over 200 draws.
The sample is the same in every row: it excludes tests whose reported coefficient is missing or zero, following Kudrin (2025), and tests whose reported standard error is zero or whose rounding unit in $t$-units, $\kappa$, exceeds 0.5. The first line of each block gives the design's numbers of $t$-statistics and articles.
\textit{Projection test}: $J_0=20$, $J=40$, and a grid of 3,000 points on $[-6.5,6.5]$ plus the point 9999; the discretization error $E(L,\delta)=0.00063$ is included in every $p$-value and in $\widehat{B}$.
$10^3\widehat{B}$: the breakdown statistic (Section 5.1), a lower 95\% confidence bound on the distance between the reported $t$-curve and the set of $t$-curves possible without selective reporting, shown under $s_{\min}$ and $s_{\min}$+noise. \textit{Rounding allowance} ($\times 10^3$, first line of each block): an upper bound on the distance that rounding can create under these treatments, 0.13 times the average of $\kappa^6$ (Propositions 3 and 4). A rejection with $\widehat{B}$ above it cannot be attributed to rounding.
$T_\infty$, $T_H$: the KS-type and histogram-based tests of Kudrin (2025), using the sinc kernel with frequency cutoff 3 and, for $T_H$, the points $0.01,0.02,\ldots,0.15$. His hybrid test $T_H^{\mathrm{hyb}}$ was run as well; $^\dagger$ marks the cells where it coincides with $T_H$ because its shape restrictions do not bind.
\textit{EKW}: the tests of Elliott et al.\ (2022), run with their Tests.R on the two-sided $p$-values $2(1-\Phi(|t|))$: the density discontinuity test at 0.05 (Disc.), the binomial test on $[0.04,0.05]$ (Binom.), and the Cox and Shi (2023) histogram-based tests on $(0,0.15]$ with 15 bins for monotonicity (CS1) and for 2-monotonicity with bounds (CS2B).
The projection test, $T_\infty$, $T_H$, CS1 and CS2B cluster by article, and Disc.\@ and Binom. do not; the projection test and $T_\infty$ use 1,000 article-level bootstrap repetitions.
Bold: $p<.05$, except that under $s_{\min}$ and $s_{\min}$+noise a projection test is bold only when its $\widehat{B}$ exceeds the rounding allowance.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rounding_appendix} reports the projection test with $J_0=30$ and without the shift, the $p$-curve test of Simonsohn et al.\ (2014), and the EKW tests LCM and Fisher.
