\footnotesize
\textit{Notes:} The table reports $p$-values for the null hypothesis of no selective reporting in the data of Brodeur et al.\ (2020), by research design (RCT: randomized controlled trials; IV: instrumental variables; DID: difference-in-differences; RDD: regression discontinuity).
Columns differ in the treatment of rounding.
\textit{None}: each $t$-statistic is the ratio of the reported coefficient and standard error, and reported $t$-statistics are used as printed.
\textit{Unif.}: the coefficient and the standard error are each replaced by an independent uniform draw from its rounding interval, and reported $t$-statistics receive uniform noise at their last digit; $p$-values are averages over 200 draws.
$s_{\min}$: each coefficient is divided by the smallest standard error consistent with the reported one, and reported $t$-statistics are used as printed (Section 5.2); this treatment is deterministic.
\textit{+noise}: each coefficient, plus uniform noise at its last digit, is divided by the smallest standard error consistent with the reported one, and reported $t$-statistics receive uniform noise at their last digit (Section 5.2); $p$-values are averages over 200 draws.
The sample is the same in every column.
It excludes tests whose reported coefficient is missing or zero, following Kudrin (2025), and tests whose reported standard error is zero or whose rounding unit in $t$-units, $\kappa$, exceeds 0.5.
$n$, \textit{Articles}: the numbers of $t$-statistics and of articles in each design's sample.
\textit{Projection test}: $J_0=20$, $J=40$, and a grid of 3,000 points on $[-6.5,6.5]$ plus the point 9999; the discretization error $E(L,\delta)=0.00063$ is included in every $p$-value and in $\widehat{B}$.
$T_\infty$, $T_H$: the KS-type and histogram-based tests of Kudrin (2025), using the sinc kernel with frequency cutoff 3 and, for $T_H$, the points $0.01,0.02,\ldots,0.15$; his hybrid test coincides with $T_H$ in every case.
\textit{EKW}: the tests of Elliott et al.\ (2022), run with their Tests.R on the two-sided $p$-values $2(1-\Phi(|t|))$, namely the least concave majorant test (LCM) and Fisher's test on $(0,0.15]$, the density discontinuity test at 0.05 (Disc.), the binomial test on $[0.04,0.05]$, and the Cox and Shi (2023) histogram-based tests on $(0,0.15]$ with 15 bins for monotonicity (CS1) and for 2-monotonicity with bounds (CS2B).
The projection test, $T_\infty$, $T_H$, CS1 and CS2B cluster by article, and LCM, Fisher, Disc.\@ and Binomial do not; the projection test and $T_\infty$ use 1,000 article-level bootstrap repetitions.
$\widehat{B}$: the breakdown statistic under $s_{\min}$ (Section 5.1), a lower 95\% confidence bound on the distance between the reported $t$-curve and the set of $t$-curves possible without selective reporting.
\textit{Rounding}: an upper bound on the distance that rounding can create under $s_{\min}$, equal to 0.13 times the average of $\kappa^6$ (Propositions 3 and 4).
A rejection with $\widehat{B}$ above this bound cannot be attributed to rounding.
