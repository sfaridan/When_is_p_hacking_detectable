5. HOW THIS SAMPLE DIFFERS FROM VAN ZWET, SCHWAB AND SENN (2021)
  Their sample: one (b, s) pair per trial, from the trial's first or primary efficacy outcome, randomized trials of any
  size; their paper reports 23,551 trials (23,747 in its arXiv version).
  Three readings of that selection, applied to this file (randomized trials, efficacy outcomes, one per trial label):
    A. each trial's first comparison and outcome in its review:          33,172 trials (14,072 with at least 100 participants)
    B. only each review's first comparison and outcome (its primary meta-analysis): 15,108 trials (7,175 with at least 100)
    C. each comparison's first outcome (a comparison usually lists its primary outcome first): 24,786 trials (11,068 with at least 100)
  Differences in our final sample:
    - every efficacy outcome of a trial, not only the first; outcomes are clustered by trial
    - only outcomes with at least 100 participants
    - continuous outcomes only
    - continuous outcomes whose rounding unit in z-units, kappa = 2a / SE_min, exceeds 0.5 are dropped
    - z: recomputed from the arm-level data
    - repeated copies of the same outcome data are dropped (same trial label, arm data and z)

6. ROUNDING OF CONTINUOUS OUTCOMES
  kappa = 2a / SE_min. a: the finer of the two means' rounding units; SE_min: the standard error from the smallest SDs
  consistent with the printed ones (s - b/2). A difference of two means each rounded to a can be off by up to a, so its
  rounding interval is 2a wide (the paper's kappa = a / s_min, for a coefficient printed to a).
  Before the cap: 22,136 continuous outcomes, 926 of them with z exactly 0 (equal rounded means). kappa, quantiles 50, 75, 90, 99%:
      50%   75%   90%   99% 
    0.120 0.382 1.016 5.916 
  Dropped by the cap of 0.5 (kappa above it, or not finite): 4,456 outcomes (20.1%), 626 of them with z exactly 0.
  Final sample: 17,680 continuous outcomes, 300 of them with z exactly 0; kappa median 0.080, maximum 0.500; rounding allowance 0.13 x mean(kappa^6) = 7.82e-05 (J0 = 20; Propositions 3 and 4).
  Uniform de-rounding draws each mean within +-1/2 of its unit and each SD from its rounding interval; the change in z is added to the outcome's z.
  Under smin each continuous outcome's z is rescaled from its printed SE to SE_min; under smin + noise, uniform noise at
  each mean's last digit is added to the numerator (the same draws as uniform). Under both, a projection-test rejection
  is certified when B-hat exceeds the subsample's rounding allowance C mean(kappa^6), C = 0.13 for J0 = 20 and 0.31 for
  J0 = 30; the mean counts dichotomous outcomes as exact (kappa = 0) and does not cover outcomes reported as an estimate
  and SE, whose rounding unit is unknown (they stay as printed under every treatment).
